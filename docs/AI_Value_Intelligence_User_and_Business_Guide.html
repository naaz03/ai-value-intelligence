1. Why this platform exists

Organizations are spending heavily on Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise, Azure OpenAI, enterprise assistants, custom agents, infrastructure, training, and governance. Senior leadership still struggles to answer whether that spend is producing measurable business value.

Typical questions this product is built to answer:

  • How much are we spending on AI, and on what?
  • Which tools are actually used, and which licenses sit idle?
  • Which departments adopt quickly, and which over-license?
  • Which use cases create operational improvement versus cash?
  • How much of Potential value becomes Demonstrated, then Realized?
  • What is portfolio ROI, payback, and net benefit?
  • What should we scale, optimize, reassess, or stop?
This release is a demonstration. All figures belong to a fictional GCC holding company, Al Noor Holdings PJSC. They are realistic and internally consistent, but they are not a real client residual and must not be presented as such.

2. What the application covers

The platform is an end-to-end AI value-management system, not a usage dashboard. It follows one chain:

InvestmentAvailabilityAdoptionEffective usage Operational improvementDemonstrated benefitFinancial realization Realized valueROI
Investment

Licenses, infrastructure, implementation, training, support, security, governance — by product, vendor, unit, and use case.

Adoption

Eligible, licensed, and active users; power / regular / occasional / inactive / never-activated / abandoned segments; training lift.

Use-case portfolio

Registry of 84 initiatives with status, risk, owners, value, ROI, and Scale / Optimize / Monitor / Reassess / Stop actions.

Productivity

Before/after task time, quality, rework, hours saved, and FTE capacity — kept separate from cash.

Value realization

Potential → Demonstrated → Realized waterfall, leakage, finance validation, and benefit type.

Optimization & decisions

Idle licenses, overlapping tools, department index, 12–36 month scenarios, and an interactive executive simulator.

The application deliberately does not treat prompts as productivity, usage as value, hours saved as cash, or adoption as ROI.

3. Getting started

Start the application

cd ai_audit source .venv/bin/activate streamlit run app.py

Open the URL Streamlit prints, typically http://localhost:8501. No API keys are required. On first launch the synthetic dataset is created automatically if it is missing.

First ten minutes

  1. Stay on Executive Overview and read the KPI strip.
  2. Set the sidebar Persona to your role. Numbers do not change; emphasis and insight order do.
  3. Leave filters empty for the group view, then restrict Date or Business unit to see the same KPIs recompute.
  4. Open Value Realization for the Potential → Demonstrated → Realized waterfall.
  5. Open License Optimization for unused-seat recommendations calculated from the register.
  6. Open AI Value Simulator and move the realization slider to see why hours are not automatically cash.

6. The AI value framework

Three value concepts are kept distinct everywhere in the product.

Potential value

Theoretical benefit from use-case assumptions at steady state, ramped by time in production. It is a planning number, not evidence.

Demonstrated value

Benefit supported by operational evidence (time saved, quality, capacity, task volume). Still not booked cash.

Realized value

Benefit captured by the enterprise after a realization factor. A subset is flagged finance-validated.

The product enforces Potential ≥ Demonstrated ≥ Realized. Conversion rates are:

Potential-to-Demonstrated % = Demonstrated / Potential Demonstrated-to-Realized % = Realized / Demonstrated Overall value realization % = Realized / Potential

Hours saved become FTE capacity using 1,880 working hours per year. Hours become money only when a realization factor is applied (default 35% in the simulator). That is how the product avoids “we saved 100,000 hours, therefore we saved $X.”

7. Page-by-page user guide

7.1 Executive Overview

Landing page for investment-committee conversations. Shows portfolio KPIs, investment versus realized value, ROI trend, the P→D→R funnel, adoption, spend and value by business unit, top and bottom use cases, a value-versus-ROI bubble matrix, and a deterministic insight engine (Key findings, Value opportunities, Optimization, Management attention, Recommended actions).

How to use it: Set persona, confirm net benefit and realization %, then open the insight tabs before drilling into a unit or product.

7.2 AI Investment

Explains the cost stack: license, infrastructure, implementation, training, support, security, and governance. Cuts spend by product, vendor, business unit, department, and use case, with a monthly cost trend and a callout of the largest driver.

How to use it: Look for infrastructure growing faster than seats (token-heavy platforms) and implementation spikes on failed or retired initiatives.

7.3 Adoption Intelligence

Separates eligible, licensed, and active populations. Segments users into Power, Regular, Occasional, Inactive, Never Activated, and Abandoned. Compares average sessions for trained versus untrained staff and adoption by department, unit, country, job level, and product.

How to use it: High licensed counts with low adoption are a commercial problem, not a success story. Filter Legal or Human Resources to see over-licensing.

7.4 Use Case Portfolio

Enterprise registry: owner, department, status, investment, risk, Potential / Demonstrated / Realized, ROI, payback. Each use case is classified:

ActionTypical signal
ScaleStrong realized value and high ROI; ready for wider rollout
OptimizePositive ROI but weak realization — fix capture, not just usage
MonitorEarly stage or balanced performance; no scale decision yet
ReassessNegative ROI or high cost with little realized value while still live
StopRetired, paused, or clearly value-destructive

The bubble chart plots realized value (x) against ROI (y); bubble size is investment; color is classification.

7.5 Productivity Intelligence

Before/after task minutes, quality scores, rework, hours released, and FTE equivalent. An on-page realization slider shows a scenario conversion of hours to money. The booked realized value on the KPI strip still comes from the benefit register, not from hours × wage.

How to use it: Use this page to discuss operational improvement. Do not copy the “if fully cashed” figure into a finance paper.

7.6 Value Realization

Waterfall: Potential → evidence adjustment → Demonstrated → realization adjustment → Realized → AI costs → Net AI value. Also shows finance-validated versus unvalidated realized value, and value by benefit type (cost avoidance, cost reduction, revenue, capacity, productivity, quality, risk).

7.7 ROI Intelligence

Portfolio and use-case ROI using realized value only. Includes net benefit, benefit-cost ratio, payback, cost per active user, cost per AI task, cost per hour saved, realized value per user, and realized value per $1 of spend.

7.8 License Optimization

A commercial page. Identifies never-activated, abandoned, 30/60/90-day inactive, and overlapping-product seats. KPIs: current annualized license spend, optimized spend, savings, and optimization %. Recommendations are generated from the filtered license register (for example, a department with 60+ day inactivity and a dollar opportunity).

7.9 Department Benchmarking

AI Value Index (0–100) ranking departments on seven min-max normalized dimensions: adoption, usage, productivity, realized value, ROI, training, and use-case success. The score is a relative ranking in the current filter, not an absolute grade. The methodology is in an expander on the page.

7.10 Forecasting

12–36 month Conservative, Base, and Accelerated scenarios for spend, realized value, ROI, and adoption. These are trend extrapolations with scenario drift — labeled as planning views, not predictions.

7.11 AI Value Simulator

Interactive executive model. Levers: license count and price, adoption, hours saved per user, hourly cost, realization %, use-case success rate, infrastructure, training, implementation, and support/security/governance. Outputs: projected cost, gross productivity value, demonstrated, realized, net benefit, ROI, payback, and break-even users.

How to use it: Hold cost fixed and move only realization % to show the committee why “hours saved” is not a P&L line.

7.12 Data Explorer

Inspect the marts and registers that power the UI, run integrity checks, sample employee-level usage, and export CSV. Use this when Finance asks “what sits under that KPI?”

9. ROI and cost methodology

Total AI Cost = License + Infrastructure + Implementation + Training + Support + Security + Governance ROI = (Realized Financial Benefit − Total AI Cost) / Total AI Cost Net benefit = Realized − Total AI Cost Benefit-cost = Realized / Total AI Cost Payback months = Total AI Cost / Average monthly realized value Cost / active user, / task, / hour saved Realized / AI user and Realized / $1 AI spend

Gross productivity value in the simulator is hours × wage. That figure is labeled as such and is reduced by use-case success rate and realization % before it is called realized value.

11. How the data is synthesized

The dataset is not a pile of independent random numbers. Generators share one deterministic seed (42) and encode relationships so analysis produces believable business patterns.

Design principles

  • Employees who complete training are more likely to activate and run deeper sessions.
  • Job family drives product mix: engineering toward GitHub Copilot; corporate functions toward M365 Copilot; customer service toward Noor Assist.
  • Frequent users save more minutes, but hours are not auto-converted to cash.
  • Realization varies by department and benefit type (Procurement cost-avoidance is captured more readily than Marketing content productivity).
  • New use cases ramp; some remain pilots, some fail, some scale.
  • July–August usage and value dip (GCC summer).
  • Company-wide training campaigns at months 8, 18, and 27 lift activation.
  • Some staff receive overlapping products; some abandon after one or two months; some never activate.
  • Executives have fewer sessions and longer session duration than analysts.
  • Azure OpenAI token volume and compute cost rise through the history.

Grain and performance

Facts are monthly, not daily. Employee-level usage is stored, then rolled into marts that the pages query. Productivity is a measured sample of users per use case, not a census of every task. That keeps the application laptop-fast while still feeling enterprise-scale (~205k usage rows, ~12k employees).

What is fictional — and what is methodologically real

Names, IDs, and dollar amounts are invented. The method is the point of the demonstration: a value chain, a three-tier value model, a complete cost stack, license hygiene, and a refusal to equate activity with money. Those methods are intended to transfer to a real enterprise when adapters replace the synthetic source.

13. Suggested demo walkthrough

A 20-minute narrative that works with executives and does not require spoiling every hidden story.

  1. Frame the problem (2 min). “We can see spend and we can see prompts. We cannot see realized value.” Show the value chain on Executive Overview.
  2. Portfolio truth (4 min). Potential $94M, realized $29M, cost $20M, ROI ~50%. Emphasize that ROI uses realized value only.
  3. Leakage (3 min). Value Realization waterfall. Ask: where did the missing $32M of demonstrated-but-unrealized benefit go?
  4. Adoption is not value (3 min). Filter Marketing: high usage, weak cash. Filter Procurement: modest adoption, strong cost-avoidance ROI.
  5. License hygiene (3 min). License Optimization — Legal and HR idle seats, overlapping Copilot + ChatGPT, dollar opportunity.
  6. Portfolio actions (3 min). Use Case Portfolio — cheap AP invoice triage versus the retired Executive briefing agent.
  7. Decision (2 min). Simulator: change realization % and license count. Close on Scale / Optimize / Stop, not “buy more seats.”

16. Exports and quality

Where practical, pages offer CSV download for filtered KPIs, the use-case registry, license opportunities, department benchmarking, the benefit register, simulator scenarios, forecasts, and executive findings.

Data Explorer runs automated checks: primary-key uniqueness, nulls, invalid dates, negative money, orphan foreign keys, status enums, and Potential ≥ Demonstrated ≥ Realized. The generated dataset is required to pass these checks. Automated tests live in tests/ (pytest -q).

18. Limitations and disclaimer

Not a client residual. Do not present Al Noor figures as another organization’s results. Do not use this guide as investment, accounting, or audit advice.
  • All money is synthetic and directional. Currency is USD for vendor-comparable AI spend.
  • Monthly grain; no true daily telemetry.
  • Productivity is sampled, not a full task census.
  • Forecasts are scenarios, not guaranteed predictions.
  • No authentication, row-level security, or write-back.
  • License optimization is an analytical opportunity, not an instruction to terminate employment.
  • FTE capacity is released time, not an automatic FTE reduction.

19. FAQ

Why is realized value so much lower than potential?

By design. Evidence is incomplete, and even evidenced hours are only partly converted into captured benefit. That is the management problem the product exists to show.

Why don’t hours saved match realized dollars?

Because they must not. Hours are operational. Cash requires a realization factor, a benefit type that finance can recognize, and often a process change (backfill, avoided hire, or reduced third-party spend).

Do persona changes alter the numbers?

No. They change which KPIs are featured first and how insights are ordered.

Can two pages disagree?

They should not. Portfolio KPIs are computed from the same use-case monthly mart. Data Explorer shows the reconciliation totals.

Is this ready for a named client?

Not as a residual. It is ready as a method demonstration, a workshop artifact, and a blueprint for a later integration program.
